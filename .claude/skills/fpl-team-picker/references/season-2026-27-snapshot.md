# 2026/27 snapshot: what the creators believed (Aug-Oct 2026, preseason to GW6)

This page is background, not advice. It records the player- and team-specific views in the 20 source videos so the principles in `SKILL.md` have concrete examples. Prices are as quoted at the time, and roles, injuries and form will have moved on, so verify everything against live data before using it. Player names come from YouTube captions and may be misspelled.

## Teams and players the creators kept coming back to

- **Arsenal: the consensus best defence.** Every video wanted at least one Arsenal defender, and most wanted 2-3.
  - Raya (£6.0m), the set-and-forget keeper.
  - Calafiori (£5.5m, later £5.7m), an attacking left-back who often comes off around 60-75 minutes; Hincapié tended to start the European games.
  - Konsa (£4.4m), signed to start at right centre-back while Saliba was out (expected back around December). Called a "cheat code" and "irresponsible not to pick".
  - Gabriel (£8.0m), the premium option (209 points last season).
  - Saka (£9.5m) took penalties when Gyökeres was off the pitch and showed strong early goal threat.
- **Man City (Maresca): Haaland plus rotation.**
  - Haaland (£15.5m) was the default captain at home to weak sides (Coventry GW3, Sunderland GW5, Ipswich GW7, Hull GW16).
  - Almost every other City player was treated as a rotation risk: Cherki, Foden, Semenyo, O'Reilly, Gvardiol, Guéhi. The exception was Elliot Anderson, a DefCon midfielder.
- **Chelsea: a no-Europe attack.**
  - João Pedro (£7.5m) was "underpriced" and owned by 93% of experts in GW1.
  - Palmer (£9.5m) was on penalties; Rogers (£7.5m) arrived from Aston Villa.
  - Tripling up on their attack for Hull at home in GW4 was the big early move.
- **Man Utd (Carrick): Bruno for the opening fixtures.**
  - Bruno Fernandes (£12m) took penalties and set pieces and was the GW1-2 captain against Hull and Ipswich. His GW2 hat-trick and assist made a 46-point captaincy for some.
  - Mbeumo played as the No. 9.
  - Their defence was widely avoided after GW2.
- **Set-and-forget midfielders.**
  - Szoboszlai (£7.0m, Liverpool): plays 90, takes penalties, free kicks and corners.
  - Pascal Groß (£5.5m, Brighton): the "Brighton version of Szoboszlai".
  - Gibbs-White (£7.9m, Forest): nailed, on penalties, no Europe.
- **Value forwards.**
  - Barry (£5.6m, Everton): the only senior striker, with xG close to Haaland's early on.
  - Calvert-Lewin (£6.0m, Leeds): on penalties and the template third forward in GW1.
  - Wissa (~£6.1m, Newcastle).
- **DefCon defenders.**
  - Muharemović (Leeds), Vušković (Brighton) and Van Hecke (£5.0m, Spurs) offered DefCon plus attacking or set-piece threat.
  - Ajer (£4.5m, Brentford) hit DefCon in about 70% of starts.
  - Sangaré (Brentford) and Anderson (City) were the DefCon midfielders.
- **Budget keepers.** Kinsky (£4.5m, Spurs, judged underrated on xG conceded) and Verbruggen (£4.5m, Brighton). No £4.0m keeper was starting at launch.
- **Teams to target.** The promoted sides were Hull, Ipswich and Coventry. Hull's early clean sheets were widely expected to regress because they conceded so many chances.

## Calendar and chip plans discussed

- The GW1 template front three was Haaland, João Pedro and Calvert-Lewin. The no-Haaland camp captained Bruno in GW1-2.
- Bench Boost: GW1 (strong home fixtures for cheap defenders) or GW2 (cheap Coventry players at home to Hull).
- Triple Captain: Haaland at home to Coventry in GW3 was the popular choice (about 9 points in practice). A minority triple-captained Palmer at home to Hull in GW4.
- Free Hit in GW3 to dodge Arsenal v Chelsea and triple up on City v Coventry.
- Wildcard windows: GW3, GW4 (the Chelsea/Hull swing and Konsa becoming a starter), and GW6-7, around the long international break between GW5 and GW6.
