## How the videos were chosen

1. 26 YouTube searches, each sorted by view count with the "this year" filter, top 60 results each: "how to write a resume", "how to write a CV", "resume tips", "resume tips 2026", "CV tips", "CV writing tips", "how to write a CV 2026", "how to make a resume", "resume writing", "resume mistakes", "CV mistakes", "ATS resume", "ATS friendly resume", "best resume format", "resume format 2026", "resume examples", "resume template", "perfect resume", "resume advice recruiter", "resume review", "resume bullet points", "resume summary", "resume with no experience", "tailor your resume", "resume that gets interviews", "how to write a resume that stands out". 699 unique videos.
2. Automatic filter on the search fields: title mentions resume/résumé/CV, 3 minutes to 2 hours long, not a livestream, title not plainly in another language. 391 remained.
3. Manual topic review, with the rules fixed before any transcript was read. **Kept:** videos mainly about how to write, structure, format or improve a CV/resume, including recruiter reviews, ATS guides and AI-assisted writing that teaches resume content. **Dropped:** other meanings of "resume" (news, music, TV recaps, Indonesian "CV" company tax); product walkthroughs (Europass forms, Overleaf, PDF-editor or phone-app tutorials, tool reviews); career stories and roadmaps where the resume is a side topic; school-exam "letter and CV" lessons; hiring notices; "projects to add to your resume"; cover-letter-only videos. 61 candidates remained, in view order (`research/candidates.json`).
4. Each candidate's page was opened in a browser to read its upload date, view count, length and spoken language (from the auto-caption track). Kept: uploaded in the window, English audio, not live, at least 3 minutes. Top 20 by views, **capped at 2 per channel** so that repetition across videos means repetition across people. Joshua Fluke and Headless Headhunter would otherwise have taken several more slots.

## How the transcripts were captured

YouTube blocked automated downloads from the cloud server this skill was built on: first a bot check, then its "unusual traffic" page. From a home PC it served the pages but refused transcript requests from a browser a program was driving (HTTP 400). So the 20 picks were listed automatically (`scripts/fetch_transcripts_browser.cjs --list`), and the transcripts were saved by hand in a normal browser from YouTube's own "Show transcript" panel, using the page's "Save transcript" favourite. That panel produced each transcript twice; the duplicate copy was removed before reading. Two transcripts were pasted into the chat instead of saved.

## Known limitations

- **Tech-heavy.** 8 of the 18 videos are about software, data or tech resumes (1, 4, 7, 10-14). Generalise their tech specifics (languages, frameworks) to "the tools and methods of your field".
- **US-centric.** Most advice assumes US/Canadian norms. Video 16 covers the resume/CV distinction and video 2 notes that local norms (such as photos in China) differ. Check the user's country.
- **Sponsored or tool-promoting videos:** 1 (the creator's own tool), 5 (Enhancv), 9 (PDFelement), 12 (the creator's own tool), 17 (a prompt pack), plus paid reviews (8) and memberships (10, 11). Read "use tool X" as "use any resume tool or AI, then check its output".
- **Statistics are quoted, not verified.** "75% of resumes are rejected by ATS" and "recruiters spend 6-7 seconds" are widely repeated industry claims. Jeff Su's figures (video 2) come from the studies he cites. Use them as context, never as facts in the user's document.
- **Caption errors.** Auto-captions garble names and terms, e.g. "Cafka" for Kafka, "doveetail" for Dovetail, "PO SACDPG" for PPO/SAC/DDPG. Video 4's "Jack Ryan format" is the creator's own wording for the common "Jake's Resume" template.
- **Transcripts are not committed** to this public repo, because they are the creators' content. To rebuild them, follow `RUN-ON-WINDOWS.md`.
