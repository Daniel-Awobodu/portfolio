# Consensus tally: which points recur, and how often

I read each of the 18 transcripts (numbered as in `sources.md`) in full and coded it against recurring themes. A video counts towards a theme when it states or clearly applies the principle, not when it merely shows an example that happens to fit. The coding is a judgement call; the per-video notes it came from stay with the local transcripts.

**Weight rules.** Channels count first, because two videos from one creator are one opinion:

| Weight | Rule | Meaning in SKILL.md |
|---|---|---|
| Core | ≥8 of 16 channels **and** ≥9 of 18 videos | Default behaviour; override only for a stated reason |
| Strong | 5-7 channels | Apply unless the situation clearly says otherwise |
| Supporting | 3-4 channels | Tie-breaker |
| Minor | ≤2 channels | Mention when relevant |

## Dissent on the Core and Strong themes

| Theme | Dissenting videos | What they say instead |
|---|---|---|
| 3 Quantify | 10, 11 (Headless Headhunter) | Don't "brag" with ratings, revenue or user counts; save them for the interview. Small numbers can read as unqualified next to a big employer's scale. Only concurrent users of infrastructure you built is worth stating. |
| 6 Plain layout | 16 (Adam) | Side boxes and highlights are fine on a resume if they help fit more in. Nobody else agrees. |
| 10 Summary | 10, 11 (Headless Headhunter) | Summaries "don't count". Use one only to explain relocation, a visa situation or an industry change. |
| 13 Cut for relevance (coursework) | 10 vs 7, 13, 18 | Headless Headhunter: "relevant coursework is never relevant". Peralta/ouckah/Resume Genius: students keep the few most relevant courses. |
| 1 Tailor per job | 10, 11 | Tailor to a job *title*, then apply quickly and widely: being in the first ~100 applicants matters as much as the document. |
| 29 Section order | 10 vs 2 | Headless Headhunter puts a relevant degree at the top even for experienced candidates; Jeff Su puts experience first unless you are a fresh graduate. |
| 26 Dates | 16 vs 3, 8, 10, 11 | Adam: give dates for everything. The others: experienced candidates can drop graduation years. |

## Not adopted

These conflict with theme 7 (honesty), which 9 channels support, so the skill never recommends them:

- Inventing an employer to cover a gap, or giving a friend as a fake manager reference (video 8).
- Changing your location on the resume to the job's city when you don't live there or aren't moving (a tool suggestion in video 1). The honest version is "Relocating to <city> by <month year>" (video 10).
- Prompting an AI to produce a ">90% match" without checking each added claim (video 6), or accepting AI-invented metrics (the sponsored demo in video 9).

## Also worth knowing (made by only one or two videos)

- Hiring-manager data cited by Jeff Su (video 2): tailored resumes got 84% more interviews; the most keyword-heavy got 21% fewer than moderately tailored ones; quantified resumes 75% more; only 6% of hiring managers let AI make the decision; 28% reject AI-heavy, low-effort resumes.
- The AI ranker only ever sees what the parser extracted, so a parse failure poisons the ranking too (video 15).
- Before/after story bullets are the most convincing kind: "the task took 1,250 ms against a 700 ms limit; I parallelised the calls and got it under 700 ms" (video 13).
- Don't present your employer's scale as your own achievement (video 13).
- A bullet that two specialists in your own field can't understand will lose a recruiter (video 7); write so "your grandparent" could follow the *why* (videos 10, 11).
- If you're applying somewhere you don't live, write "Moving to X by <date>"; "willing to relocate" reads as a red flag (video 10).
- Keep two versions when a credential only matters to some employers, e.g. a security clearance for government roles (video 10).
- File names like `Name_Resume_Company.pdf`, so you know which version a recruiter saw (video 4).
- Leave "Open to work" off LinkedIn while you're employed (video 8).
- Use your resume to rehearse the interview questions it will prompt, especially about gaps and transitions (video 17).
