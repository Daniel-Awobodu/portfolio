# Templates and worked examples

All examples here are illustrative, written for this skill rather than copied from the videos. **Every number in a real resume must come from the user.** When the user hasn't given one, write `[number?]` and ask.

## 1. Brain-dump questions (ask per role or project)

Get the raw facts first; wording comes later (themes 7, 14). Messy answers are fine.

1. What was the situation or problem when you started? Who was affected?
2. What did *you* do (not the team)? What did you decide, build, change, fix, lead or own?
3. How: which tools, methods, software, equipment, techniques?
4. What changed because of it? Time saved, money saved or earned, errors cut, quality or satisfaction up, volume handled, speed, growth. A rough figure you can explain is fine.
5. Scale: how many people, customers, users, students, patients, sites, transactions, what budget?
6. Who noticed? Awards, promotions, being asked to train others, being given more responsibility.
7. Which of these does the target job care about most?

If the user can't put a number on something, use scale and specifics instead (how many, how often, for whom, what size). A clear, concrete bullet with no percentage beats an invented one.

## 2. Keyword map (for tailoring)

Fill one in from the job posting before rewriting anything (theme 1).

| Posting term (exact wording) | Times it appears | Must-have or nice-to-have | Where the resume proves it | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| e.g. "stakeholder management" | 3 | must | Role 1, bullet 2 | ✓ mirrored |
| e.g. "SQL" | 2 | must | (only in skills list) | add to a bullet |
| e.g. "Tableau" | 1 | nice | (none: user hasn't used it) | honest gap, leave out |

Rules:
- Use the posting's wording when it describes the user's real experience; don't use a synonym ("stakeholder management", not "cross-functional collaboration").
- State it, don't imply it: list both the specific tool and the general term the posting uses (e.g. "PostgreSQL (SQL)").
- Put the terms inside bullets, not only in the skills list.
- Never add a term the user can't back up. Gaps stay gaps; mention them to the user instead.

### Teen / first job

Contact (name, phone, professional email, city) → short objective (who you are, the job, why you'd be good at it) → Education → Experience (babysitting, volunteering, clubs, team sports, family business, school projects, as long as you really did them regularly) → Skills (concrete ones: software, languages, certifications such as first aid, a driving licence if relevant). One page.

Formatting for all of them: one column; no tables, text boxes, icons, photos, skill bars or graphics (unless a photo is the local norm). Contact details go in the body, not the page header or footer. Standard font (Calibri, Arial, Helvetica, Times New Roman, or similar) at 10.5-12 pt; normal margins; consistent date format, alignment and punctuation. Export a PDF with selectable text under ~2 MB, named like `First-Last-Resume-Company.pdf`, unless the employer asks for Word.

## 4. Bullet rewrites (before → after)

Patterns used (theme 2): **XYZ** "Accomplished X, as measured by Y, by doing Z"; **action-context-result**; **what + how + why + where** (the plain-English business reason, under a real role or project); **decision + outcome**.

| Field | Before (duty / vague) | After (achievement; numbers must be the user's) |
|---|---|---|
| Customer support | Handled customer emails and queries. | Answered ~60 customer emails a day in Zendesk and wrote 12 help articles for the most common questions, cutting repeat tickets by [number?]% in one quarter. |
| Retail | Worked the till and stocked shelves. | Reorganised the stockroom by sales volume so Saturday restocks took ~20 minutes instead of ~45; trusted with closing and cash-up after three months. |
| Teaching | Taught Year 9 maths. | Taught maths to four Year 9 classes (120 students); introduced weekly low-stakes quizzes and raised the end-of-year pass rate from [x]% to [y]%. |
| Marketing | Managed the company's social media. | Planned and published 5 posts a week on Instagram and LinkedIn; a switch to short how-to videos lifted engagement from [x]% to [y]% over six months. |
| Software | Worked on backend APIs. | Built a REST API in Python (FastAPI) and PostgreSQL that lets the sales team pull usage reports themselves, replacing a weekly manual export that took ~3 hours. |
| Nursing | Cared for patients on the ward. | Cared for 5-6 post-surgical patients per shift on a 28-bed ward; led the move to bedside handovers, cutting handover time by ~10 minutes a shift. |
| Finance/admin | Processed invoices. | Processed 400+ supplier invoices a month in SAP; set up a duplicate-invoice check that stopped [£/$ amount?] of double payments in its first quarter. |
| Student society | Helped organise events. | Organised three careers evenings a term for the economics society: booked speakers from 9 local employers, ran sign-ups and managed a £600 budget. |
| Internship (teamwork shown, not claimed) | Team player who takes feedback well. | Built two features for the mobile app in a team of four, with every change going through code review; addressed reviewer comments within a day. |

Checks for every bullet:
- Does it start with a strong past-tense verb (present tense for the current role)? Is no verb used three or more times?
- Does it say what changed, not just what the job was? Would a reader outside the user's team understand it?
- Is the most important keyword for the target job in it, and for the first role, in the first bullet?
- Is every number real, believable for the company's size, and explainable in an interview?
- Is it 1-2 lines, without a last line of one or two words?

## 5. Summaries (2-3 lines, tailored)

- **Experienced:** "Operations analyst with 6 years in e-commerce logistics. Redesigned returns routing across 3 warehouses, cutting processing time by [x]%. Brings SQL and Power BI skills and a habit of turning messy processes into simple dashboards."
- **Career changer:** "Former secondary-school science teacher moving into instructional design. Built and ran a blended-learning unit used by 240 students across the department. Certified in Articulate 360 (2026); portfolio of 4 e-learning modules linked above."
- **New graduate:** "Computer science graduate (2026) with two internships in backend development. Shipped an internal tool used by 40 engineers at [Company]. Strongest in Java, Spring Boot and PostgreSQL."
- **Entry-level objective:** "Sixth-form student looking for weekend work at a garden centre. Reliable with customers from two years of volunteering at the local food bank, and comfortable lifting and outdoor work."

Avoid: "results-driven", "dynamic", "proven track record", "passionate", "team player", "hard-working", "detail-oriented", "go-getter", "thinks outside the box", "spearheaded cross-functional initiatives", "leveraged synergies". Prove the trait in a bullet, or delete it.

## 6. Skills section (grouped, evidenced)

```
SKILLS
Data & analysis: SQL (PostgreSQL), Excel (pivot tables, Power Query), Power BI
Programming: Python (pandas), R
Methods: A/B testing, cohort analysis, forecasting
Languages: English (native), French (professional working)
```

Order the categories by what the target job emphasises. List only hard skills and tools, and only ones the user has really used; each should also appear in at least one bullet. No soft-skill lists, no proficiency bars or stars, and no languages below intermediate level.

## 7. Academic CV (for academic jobs, PhD/grad-school applications, research fellowships, medical residencies)

This is different from a "CV" in the UK/Europe/Africa/Asia sense, which just means a resume. An academic CV is a full record, and has no page limit if everything in it is relevant:

Contact · Research interests/objective · Education · Academic appointments · Publications (peer-reviewed first; full citations) · Conference presentations and talks · Grants, fellowships and funding · Awards and honours · Teaching experience (courses, level, role) · Research experience (labs, fieldwork, methods) · Supervision/mentoring · Service (reviewing, committees) · Professional memberships · Languages and technical skills · References (usually 2-3 with contact details).

Reverse chronological within each section, with dates on everything. Plain formatting still applies.

## 8. Review scorecard

**20-second skim first** (theme 5). Without reading closely, can you tell:
1. What job this person is going for, and at what level?
2. Their most recent or most relevant role, and whether it fits?
3. One concrete, impressive result?
4. The two or three must-have skills from the posting, shown in context?

Then score each Core law 0-2 (0 = missing, 1 = partly, 2 = done):

| Core law | 0-2 | Evidence / fix |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Tailored to the target, in its exact words | | |
| 2 Achievements, not duties | | |
| 3 Quantified, believably | | |
| 4 Specific, no generic filler | | |
| 5 Fit visible in a skim (front-loaded) | | |
| 6 Plain, readable, parsable layout | | |
| 7 Honest and defensible | | |
| **Total /14** | | |

12-14: ready after polish. 8-11: worth a focused rewrite of the weakest areas. Below 8: rebuild from a brain dump. Then list the Strong checks that failed (length, headings, summary, concise bullets, contact/links, relevance cuts, verbs, proofreading, skills proven, PDF, bullets per role).

## 9. Before you send

- [ ] Every claim true, and the user can explain each one in an interview
- [ ] Posting's must-have terms appear in bullets (keyword map done)
- [ ] One column, standard headings, contact details in the body, no tables or graphics
- [ ] Length right for career stage (1 page early career; 2 at most for experienced)
- [ ] Phone dialable, email professional and working, links tested
- [ ] Spell-checked, read aloud, checked by a second person if possible
- [ ] PDF with selectable text (try highlighting it), small file, sensible name
- [ ] Dates, titles and employers match LinkedIn
- [ ] Application form answers (location, work authorisation, years of experience) are accurate
