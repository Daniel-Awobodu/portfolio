## The problem

Good cold outreach takes time, and a B2B agency selling automation services
felt it. For every company, someone has to research it, find the right person
to contact, look for a timely reason to get in touch, and write an email that
doesn't read like a template. Done by hand, that is slow. Done by a careless
AI, it produces confident emails built on made-up facts, sent to the wrong
person.

## What I built

Two connected workflows in n8n. One researches and drafts. The other lets a
person approve, reject or improve each draft from Slack.

![The research and drafting workflow in n8n: fetch new companies, enrich them, find and score contacts, search the news, find a hook, draft the email and post it to Slack](/case-studies/ai-outreach/workflow-research.png)

*The research and drafting workflow, run every day. The branch below the main
line flags companies for manual review.*

1. **It picks up new prospects.** Every day it collects up to 20 companies
   marked "New" in HubSpot.
2. **It researches each company.** Apollo fills in the industry, size, revenue
   and a short description, and HubSpot is updated.
3. **It finds the right person.** Hunter.io lists the people at the company,
   and the workflow scores them, favouring operations, IT and leadership
   roles. Assistants, coordinators and interns are never picked.
4. **It looks for a real reason to write.** It searches the last month of news
   about the company, and AI picks out one specific, relevant event, such as a
   funding round, a new leader or a big expansion.
5. **It drafts the email** in 120 words or fewer, opening with that event, and
   saves it in HubSpot.
6. **A person reviews it in Slack.** Each draft has a Respond button that
   opens a choice to approve or reject. Approve, and the email goes out
   through Gmail and the contact is marked Sent. Reject, and a box asks the
   reviewer why.

## How it works, explained simply

The system does the legwork of a researcher and a copywriter, then hands every
email to a person before anything is sent.

- **No email goes out without a yes.** The only path to Gmail runs through a
  person choosing Approve in Slack.
- **No reason is better than a fake one.** The AI is told to ignore stock
  news, lawsuits, awards and generic press releases, and to reject any reason
  that needs a guess to connect it to the email. When there is nothing real,
  it says so, and the email opens with a plain observation about the company
  instead of pretending something just happened.
- **It never invents proof.** The drafting rules ban made-up case studies,
  statistics and client results, along with lines like "I hope this email
  finds you well".
- **Feedback fixes the actual problem.** A rejection comes with a reason,
  such as "change the subject". The rewrite is told to fix only what the
  reviewer flagged and leave the rest alone.
- **It knows when to stop.** After two rewrites, a draft that is still
  rejected goes to a person, marked Needs Manual Review, instead of looping
  forever.
- **Gaps are flagged, not guessed.** A company with no website, one Apollo
  cannot find, or one with no suitable contact is marked for manual review.

## The outcome

Six test companies went through the system. Four were researched and matched
to a decision-maker. The other two were flagged for a person: one had no
website address on record, and Apollo could not find the other.

![HubSpot companies after a test run: four enriched with industry, revenue, size, description and a personalisation hook, and two marked Needs Manual Review](/case-studies/ai-outreach/hubspot-companies.png)

*The companies in HubSpot after the test run, each with its status and the
personalisation hook the AI found. Contact names are blurred.*

Three emails were approved and sent. One of them had its subject line
rejected first and was rewritten before approval. The fourth draft reached
the rewrite limit and went to a person.
