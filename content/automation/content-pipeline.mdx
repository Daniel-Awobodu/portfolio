## The problem

[CLIENT NAME] knew exactly what they wanted to say and never had time to say it.
Content happened in bursts — a good week, then three silent ones. Hiring a
writer had not worked, because the writer did not have the founder's context and
every draft came back needing a rewrite.

## What I built

A pipeline that starts with the one thing the founder will reliably do: talk.

They record a voice note. **n8n** picks it up, transcribes it, and passes the
transcript to the **Claude API** with a brief that holds their tone rules,
their audience, and the formats that work on each platform. The model returns a
set of drafts — a long-form post, a short one, a carousel outline, and a few
hooks — which land in **Airtable** as cards.

Nothing publishes automatically. The founder opens Airtable, approves or edits,
and approved cards flow to **Buffer** for scheduling. The approval step is
deliberate: this is a drafting system, not a ghostwriter.

## How it works, explained simply

Imagine an assistant sitting in on every conversation you have about your work.

You talk for two minutes about something you noticed this week. The assistant
takes notes, then writes up that idea several different ways — a long version, a
short version, a version that opens with a question. They bring all of it back
to your desk in a folder.

You read the folder, cross out what does not sound like you, tick what does. The
assistant then posts only the ticked ones, at the right times.

You never stare at a blank page. You only ever edit.

## The outcome

Content time went from [about 6 hours] a week to [under 45 minutes], and — the
part that actually mattered — publishing became consistent instead of bursty.
Roughly [X out of 12] drafts get approved as-is each week.
