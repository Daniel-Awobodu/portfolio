## The problem

A pet care blog wanted consistent SEO content, but writing, illustrating,
publishing and sharing every article by hand was too slow. It also wanted help
deciding what to write next, based on what people were already searching for.

## What I built

Two workflows in n8n, sharing one Google Sheet of keywords.

![The two n8n workflows: the hourly writing and publishing workflow on top, and the weekly Search Console scan below](/case-studies/content-pipeline/workflow.png)

*Top: the hourly workflow that writes, publishes and shares. Bottom: the
weekly Search Console scan.*

1. **It takes one keyword at a time.** Every hour, it picks the next new
   keyword in the sheet and marks it Processing, so it is never picked twice.
2. **It checks the topic is new.** If the blog already has a post on it, the
   keyword is marked Duplicate with the reason, and nothing is written.
3. **AI writes everything in one go:** the article, links to related posts on
   the blog, an image prompt, an X post and a LinkedIn post.
4. **The article is checked.** It needs at least 800 words, a title, summary
   and web address of the right length, and social posts that fit each
   platform. Anything that fails is marked Failed instead of going out.
5. **AI creates the featured image.** It is converted to a light WebP file and
   uploaded to WordPress with the article, saved as a draft.
6. **A person approves it in Slack.** The message links to a preview of the
   draft. Publish, and the article goes live and is shared to X and LinkedIn
   through Buffer. Abort, and the sheet records it.

![The WordPress editor showing the published article, with its AI-generated featured image, excerpt and a 913-word count](/case-studies/content-pipeline/wordpress-post.png)

*A finished article in WordPress, with its featured image, summary and word
count, published after approval.*

Every Monday, a second workflow asks Google Search Console which searches the
blog already appears for, but outside the top results. It drops keywords that
are already covered, already queued or searched too rarely, then adds up
to five of the best to the sheet. A Slack summary says what it kept and why it dropped
the rest.

## How it works, explained simply

It does the work of a small content team: a researcher, a writer, an
illustrator and a social media assistant. A person stays the editor and signs
off every post.

- **It never writes the same article twice.** Before writing, it compares
  each keyword with every published post. In testing it caught two: one
  keyword appeared word for word in an existing post's summary, and another
  overlapped 83% with a post's title.
- **Internal links always work.** The AI may only link to posts that really
  exist on the blog. Any other link it writes is removed before the draft is
  saved, so readers never land on a broken or invented page.
- **Quality is checked, not hoped for.** An article that is too short, has
  missing parts, or comes with a social post too long for the platform is
  stopped and marked Failed.
- **One article, written for each platform.** The X post is short and
  direct. The LinkedIn post is longer and more conversational.
- **It checks the draft before publishing.** If someone already published or
  deleted the draft by hand, it stops instead of posting twice.
- **New keywords are picked on evidence.** The weekly scan ranks keywords by
  how many people see the blog in search without clicking, so the list fills
  with real opportunities, not guesses.

## The outcome

A keyword typed into a sheet becomes a published article with its own image
and internal links, shared on X and LinkedIn, with one approval in between.
