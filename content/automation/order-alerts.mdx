## The problem

Quality-Buy runs Facebook ads to product pages, and every order a customer
places arrives as an email in our Gmail inbox. That inbox was the weak link.

Gmail notifications on the phone came late, so the only way to
catch an order was to keep opening Gmail. Some orders were seen up to two
hours after the customer placed them. Each one then had to be copied and sent
to my partner on WhatsApp by hand, so he could call the customer and confirm
the order. A few orders slipped through the cracks completely.

In this business, speed matters. A buyer who orders from an ad is keen right
then. Every hour before someone confirms is an hour for that buyer to cool off
or change their mind.

## What I built

A two-step automation in Zapier that connects Gmail to WhatsApp.

![The Zapier automation: Gmail checks for new order emails every 15 minutes, then sends a WhatsApp message](/case-studies/order-alerts/zap.png)

*The whole automation. Step 1 watches Gmail for new order emails. Step 2 sends
the order to WhatsApp.*

1. **Watch for new orders.** Zapier checks the inbox every 15 minutes for
   emails that match our order search, so only real orders trigger it.
2. **Send the order to WhatsApp.** The order details from the email go
   straight to my partner's WhatsApp as a message: the customer's name,
   delivery address, phone numbers, state, product and price.

## How it works, explained simply

Think of it as a colleague whose only job is to check the inbox every 15
minutes and forward each new order to your phone.

- **WhatsApp is the one app that always gets seen.** Email can wait for hours.
  A WhatsApp message gets read in minutes, so that is where orders now go.
- **Only orders get through.** The search filter means newsletters and other
  emails are ignored. Nothing but real orders reaches WhatsApp.
- **Honest about timing.** It is not instant. Zapier checks Gmail on a
  15-minute cycle, so an order reaches us within 15 minutes. That was more
  than enough to fix a two-hour gap.

## The outcome

Every new order now lands in my partner's WhatsApp within about 15 minutes of
the customer placing it, instead of up to two hours. Nobody has to keep
checking Gmail, and no order slips through anymore.

Orders get confirmed while the buyer is still keen. Every order is cash on
delivery, so that confirmation call is the moment that decides whether the
sale actually closes.

This was my first live automation. It is only two steps, and that is the
point: not every problem needs a complex system. Sometimes the fix is
connecting two tools that should already have been talking to each other.
