## The problem

A dental group with branches across Nigeria kept missing calls during busy
hours. Patients who reach no one rarely leave a voicemail, and most never call
back. Every missed ring was a booking lost to another clinic.

## What I built

A phone receptionist built with Vapi, backed by an n8n workflow. It is the
phone version of the
[website chat assistant](/automation/dental-chatbot) I built for the same
practice.

![The Vapi assistant for STACKEDHQ Dentals: the speech, language and voice models, with an estimated cost of about 12 cents a minute](/case-studies/voice-receptionist/vapi-assistant.png)

*The assistant in Vapi. It hears the caller with a medical speech model,
thinks with GPT-4.1 and speaks with a natural ElevenLabs voice.*

1. **It answers the call** and finds out whether the caller wants to book,
   has a question, or has an emergency.
2. **It answers questions** about hours, services, prices, HMOs and locations
   from the clinic's own information.
3. **It checks real availability.** It asks which branch, what the visit is
   for, and when suits them, then reads out the first three open times from
   that branch's calendar.
4. **It books the slot** once the caller picks a time, after reading their
   name and phone number back to confirm them.
5. **It texts a confirmation** with the branch, date and time.
6. **Emergencies go straight to a person.** The call is transferred to the
   clinic's phone instead of being handled by the agent.

![The n8n workflow behind the agent: one webhook, a switch that routes to checking availability or booking, a recheck before booking, then the confirmation text](/case-studies/voice-receptionist/workflow.png)

*The workflow behind the agent. Top: checking availability. Below: the recheck
before booking, which either books the slot and sends the text, or offers new
times if the slot was just taken.*

## How it works, explained simply

It answers like a receptionist who never misses a ring, knows every branch's
diary, and double checks it before promising a time.

- **Every branch has its own calendar.** A Google Sheet links each branch to
  its calendar, so an Abuja booking can never land in the Lagos diary.
- **It works like the clinic does.** Appointments are one hour, mornings run
  from 8am to 1pm, afternoons from 2pm to 6pm, and the 1pm lunch break is
  never offered. If the day the caller wants is full, it looks up to six days
  ahead.
- **A slot is rechecked right before booking.** A staff member could fill the
  same time while the caller is still talking. I tested exactly that: mid-call,
  I moved another appointment into the 5pm slot the caller had chosen. The
  agent caught it, said 5pm was no longer free, and offered three new times.
  The caller picked 4pm, and that is what was booked.
- **It reads details back.** During testing, the agent got the caller's phone
  number wrong. Because it read the number back, the caller corrected it, and
  the booking used the right number.
- **It sounds like a front desk.** Soft office sounds play in the background,
  and the confirmation text is sent a short while after the call, so it reads
  like a message from the front desk rather than an instant robot reply.

## The outcome

Every call gets answered. Questions are handled on the spot, and bookings go
straight into the right branch's calendar with the patient's details, even
when the calendar changes mid-call.
