## The problem

At a bakery in England, customers ordered on WhatsApp in messy free text, like
"can I get 2 loaves and a dozen buns for Friday". Staff had to work out the
items, quantities and pickup day by hand before writing each order down, and
every unclear message meant a round of back and forth.

## What I built

A WhatsApp ordering assistant in n8n, with Airtable as the bakery's order
book.

![A WhatsApp chat with the bakery: one message asking for sourdough on Tuesday, sausage rolls on Thursday and chelsea buns on Saturday becomes three orders with prices, then all three are confirmed with order numbers](/case-studies/whatsapp-bakery/chat-multi-order.png)

*One message, three pickup days. The bot splits it into three orders with
prices, waits for YES, then confirms each with its own order number.*

1. **It reads text and voice notes.** Voice notes are transcribed first.
   Stickers and images get a polite "text or voice only" reply.
2. **Simple messages never reach the AI.** Plain rules answer greetings,
   opening hours, the address, the menu, YES, NO, cancel and "my orders",
   instantly and at no AI cost.
3. **AI reads the order.** It turns the message into items, quantities and
   pickup dates, using the real menu (including the names people actually
   use, like "bap" or "barm") and a calendar of the next three weeks. One
   message can hold up to three orders for different days.
4. **Code checks every detail.** Each item must be on the menu and in the
   customer's own words. Prices come from the menu, never from the AI. The
   weekday must match the date, and the bakery must be open with enough
   notice.
5. **It asks when unsure.** Which bread? How many? Which day? It asks up to
   three questions at once, and remembers the half-finished order while the
   customer replies.
6. **Nothing is final without a YES.** The customer sees a summary with
   prices, and the order is only confirmed when they reply YES.

## How it works, explained simply

The bot behaves like a patient counter assistant: it reads every message
twice, never guesses, and writes everything in the order book.

- **It never guesses a quantity.** "A dozen" is 12 and "a couple" is 2, but
  no number means it asks.
- **It knows the bakery's week.** Ask for a Sunday and it offers the next open
  day. A Victoria Sponge needs two days' notice, and the bot knows that too.
- **It is honest about the menu.** Ask for something the bakery doesn't sell,
  and it says so instead of swapping in something else.
- **Changes are safe.** A change to a confirmed order stays a proposal until
  the customer replies YES, and the original order stands until then.
- **Late changes go to a person.** After 4pm the day before pickup, the order
  may already be in the oven, so cancels and changes are emailed to staff
  instead of being done by the bot.
- **Nothing is ever deleted.** Cancelled orders are marked Cancelled, and
  orders nobody confirms within 48 hours are marked Expired.
- **It can't be tricked into doing things twice.** A repeated message is
  ignored, and a second YES can't confirm an order twice.

## The outcome

Customers order the way they naturally write and speak, and the bakery gets
clean, priced, confirmed orders in Airtable without anyone retyping a
message.

![The Airtable message log: each message with its type, route (simple rules or AI), outcome, order ID and the AI tokens it used](/case-studies/whatsapp-bakery/message-log.png)

*Every message is logged with the route it took and what it cost. Messages
handled by simple rules used no AI at all. The WhatsApp number is blurred.*

Every evening at 6pm, staff get tomorrow's bake list by email, with item
totals and a list of pickups. In the same run, orders nobody confirmed are marked
Expired. If any step ever fails, a separate workflow emails staff the name of
the failed step and its likely cause.

![The 6pm workflows: one marks unconfirmed orders as expired and resets stale conversations, the other builds and emails tomorrow's bake list](/case-studies/whatsapp-bakery/evening-jobs.png)

*The evening jobs. Top: tidying up unconfirmed orders. Bottom: tomorrow's bake
list, sent by email.*

Tested live on WhatsApp, using Meta's test number. Testing caught real
problems, and each was fixed. For example, a bare "cancel" once cancelled a
customer's only order. Now the bot lists their orders and asks which one.
