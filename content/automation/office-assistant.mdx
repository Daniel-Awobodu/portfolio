## The problem

This is an industry build: a complete, working system built to a realistic
brief from a marketing agency.

The agency was losing hours every week to email triage, filing invoices,
scheduling requests and remembering follow-ups. They wanted one assistant
across their Google Workspace instead of hiring an admin.

## What I built

An AI assistant in n8n that reads every new email, decides what it is, and
acts on it in Gmail, Google Drive and Google Calendar.

![The n8n workflow: Gmail trigger, attachment reader for PDFs and images, one AI classification step, a check on its answer, then lanes for filing invoices in Drive, labelling, starring urgent mail and creating calendar reminders](/case-studies/office-assistant/workflow.png)

*The workflow. Left: the email and its attachment are read. Middle: one AI
step sorts it, and code checks the answer. Right: each lane acts in Drive,
Gmail or Calendar. Click the image to see it full size.*

1. **It checks the inbox every 5 minutes**, skipping promotions, social and
   update emails.
2. **It reads the attachment.** PDFs are read directly, and images are read
   by AI, so an invoice that arrives as a photo still counts.
3. **One AI step sorts the email** into a lane: invoice, scheduling or
   general. It also sets two separate flags, urgent and needs follow-up,
   writes a one-line summary, and names the vendor on invoices.
4. **Code checks the AI's answer.** The lane must be one of the three and
   the flags must be clear yes or no answers. Anything else stops the run
   instead of filing something in the wrong place.
5. **Each lane acts on it.** Invoices are filed in a Drive folder for that
   vendor, created on their first invoice. Scheduling and general emails are
   labelled. Urgent emails are starred and labelled. Follow-ups get a
   calendar reminder at the next 9am or 2pm slot.

## How it works, explained simply

Think of it as an office assistant who opens every email first and puts it
on the right pile, with the urgent ones on top.

- **Invoices come first.** One test email attached an invoice, asked for a
  revised deck by 2pm and asked to move a meeting. The invoice decided the
  lane, and the rest became urgent and follow-up flags, so nothing was lost.
- **A PDF is not automatically an invoice.** A signed contract PDF and a
  campaign report spreadsheet both went to General, because no money was
  being billed.
- **"Let's grab coffee sometime" isn't a meeting request.** Only a real
  request to book or move a meeting goes to Scheduling.
- **Urgency comes from what's asked, not the subject line.** A deadline like
  "by 2pm today" makes an email urgent. The word "URGENT" alone doesn't.
- **One vendor, one folder.** Names are cleaned up, so "Acme Co." becomes
  "Acme", and the assistant looks for the vendor's folder before creating a
  new one.
- **It never sends anything.** It only labels, stars, files and adds
  reminders, so nothing goes out in the agency's name.

## The outcome

Every email arrives already sorted, invoices file themselves, and the urgent
ones are starred at the top.

![Gmail after a test run: each email labelled AI/General, AI/Scheduling or AI/Urgent, with urgent emails starred, and the AI labels in the sidebar](/case-studies/office-assistant/gmail-labels.png)

*The test inbox after a run. The emergency reschedule went to Scheduling and

![Google Drive: an Invoices folder with a folder for each vendor (Acme, Webflow, Luminary Print Co), and inside Luminary Print Co, the invoice that arrived as an image](/case-studies/office-assistant/drive-folders.png)

*A folder per vendor, created automatically. Below: an invoice that arrived
as an image, read by AI and filed in its vendor's folder.*

It also reads invoices accurately. From one PDF, it picked up the full total
of $132.22, made up of a $123.00 subtotal plus 7.5% tax.

Two known gaps, stated plainly. The follow-up flag is too eager: in testing
it set reminders on emails that didn't need one, like a casual catch-up note.
And attachments are paired with their vendor folder by position, which is
only reliable when one email arrives at a time. Tightening the follow-up rule
and matching each file to its email are the next two fixes.
