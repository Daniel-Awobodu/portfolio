## The problem

This is an industry build: a complete, working system built to a realistic
brief from a supplier of surgical, laboratory and diagnostic equipment.

Customers email their quote requests, often with the items spread across
attached files. For every request, a staff member opened the email and each
attachment, picked out the items and quantities, looked up every price,
checked the deadline, and typed the reply by hand.

## What I built

A workflow in n8n that takes a request from the inbox to a priced reply,
with Airtable holding the product catalog and every request.

![A quote request email that lists no items itself, with five attachments: an image, two text files, a CSV spreadsheet and a PDF](/case-studies/quote-requests/request-email.png)

*A test request. The email itself lists no items: all 12 are spread across
five attachments, including a photo, two text files, a spreadsheet and a
PDF.*

1. **It picks up new requests.** Every two minutes, it checks the quotes
   inbox and skips any email it has already handled.
2. **It reads every attachment.** PDFs, text files, and CSV and Excel
   spreadsheets are read directly, and photos are read by AI. Each file is
   handled in turn.
3. **AI pulls out the request:** every item exactly as the customer wrote
   it, the quantity, the unit, any model number, and the deadline. An item
   mentioned in two files is only counted once.
4. **Every item is matched to the catalog.** The AI searches the product
   catalog in Airtable, by model number first and then by name. Each match
   gets a confidence rating, and the price always comes from the catalog.
5. **Everything is recorded.** The request and each priced line item are
   saved in Airtable, along with the file each item came from.
6. **A reply is drafted for approval.** A Gmail draft is written, and Slack
   shows a summary of every item, its match and the subtotal. Approve, and
   the reply goes out in the same email thread. Reject, and the request is
   flagged for manual review.

## How it works, explained simply

Think of it as a sales assistant who opens every attachment, checks every
price against the price list, and leaves the finished reply on your desk to
sign.

- **It never invents a price.** Prices only come from the catalog. If an item
  isn't in the catalog, the reply marks it "to be confirmed", leaves it out of
  the subtotal, and the request is flagged for review.
- **It doesn't promise what it can't back up.** The test request asked for
  delivery costs and VAT, which aren't in the catalog, so the reply says they
  will be confirmed instead of making up a figure.
- **The customer's words are kept.** Each line shows the item exactly as the
  customer wrote it, beside the catalog product it was matched to, so staff
  can check every match at a glance.
- **Model numbers come first.** When a customer gives a catalog number, that
  is searched before anything else, so "DE-BAL-01" finds the exact balance.
- **Deadlines are never made up.** A clear date is recorded. "ASAP" is marked
  as vague, and no deadline stays empty instead of becoming a guessed date.
- **Nothing goes out unapproved.** If nobody responds in Slack within two
  days, the reply stays a draft and the request is marked for manual review.

## The outcome

In testing, a request with no items in the email and five attachments became
12 priced line items and a ready-to-send reply, without anyone opening a
file. Once it was approved, the reply went out in the customer's email
thread.

![Line items in Airtable grouped by request, each showing the customer's wording, the file it came from, the matched product, the match confidence, the unit price and the line total](/case-studies/quote-requests/line-items.png)

*Line items in Airtable, grouped by request. Each one shows the customer's
wording, which file it came from, the catalog match, how confident the match
is, and the price.*

![The end of the approved reply: the last items, a subtotal of 1,784,500 naira excluding delivery and VAT, and notes saying delivery and VAT will be confirmed](/case-studies/quote-requests/reply-sent.png)

*The end of the approved reply: the last of the 12 items, the ₦1,784,500
subtotal, and notes that only promise what the catalog can back up.*

Other test requests checked the safety rules. One included items the
supplier doesn't stock: they were marked to be confirmed, left out of the
subtotal, and the request was flagged for manual review. Another, with no
attachments and nothing from the catalog, was flagged the same way, and no
line items were created.

One known gap, stated plainly: Word documents are not read yet, so an item
that only appears in a Word file would be missed. Adding Word support is the
next thing I would add for a supplier running this live.
