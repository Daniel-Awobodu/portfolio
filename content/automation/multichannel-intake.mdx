## The problem

This is an industry build: a complete, working system built to a realistic
brief from a healthcare advisory firm, a team of consultants that hospitals,
clinics and medical groups hire for advice on regulation, billing and
operations.

Requests arrived three ways: the website form, email, and notes typed by
staff after a call or meeting. For every one, a staff member had to copy it
into a table, decide what kind of request it was, work out the next step and
write the first reply by hand.

## What I built

A workflow in n8n that turns a request from any of the three channels into
the same structured record in Airtable, then classifies it and drafts the
reply.

![The website form where clients request a consultation: name, email, organization and their request](/case-studies/multichannel-intake/web-form.png)

*The website form. Clients get a reference number on screen as soon as they
send it, to quote in any follow-up.*

1. **It takes requests from three places.** The website form, an email
   inbox, and a form staff fill in after a phone call or meeting.
2. **It turns them into one shape.** Each request becomes the same record,
   with the source, the requester, the original wording and a reference
   number. For emails, quoted earlier messages and signatures are removed,
   and the organisation is worked out from the email domain.
3. **It filters out junk.** Bots are quietly ignored, and a form with an
   empty name, a broken email address or too short a request is sent back to
   be corrected.
4. **It skips duplicates.** On the web form and staff notes, the same
   request from the same person on the same day is caught before it is
   saved, even if the punctuation or spacing changes. An email is never
   logged twice.
5. **It saves the request first, then asks the AI.** The AI gives each
   request a title, a category, an urgency, a suggested next step for the
   advisor, a drafted holding reply and a confidence score.
6. **Urgent requests alert the team.** High and Critical requests post to
   Slack with a link to the record. Normal and Low ones wait in the table
   for the day's review.

## How it works, explained simply

Think of it as a front office coordinator who logs every request the moment
it arrives, wherever it came from, then reads it and puts it on the right
advisor's desk.

- **Nothing is lost if the AI fails.** The request is saved before the AI
  runs, and the original wording is never overwritten. If classification
  fails, the record is marked as failed with the reason, and the team is
  told on Slack.
- **The AI only picks from approved options.** There are eight categories
  and four urgency levels. If the AI returns anything else, code replaces it
  with "Other / Unclear" or "Normal" and notes what happened.
- **Critical means critical.** Only patient safety or active regulatory
  enforcement counts as Critical. A tight deadline is High, so the alert
  channel isn't flooded.
- **The drafted reply never gives advice.** It acknowledges the request,
  restates it so the client knows they were understood, and says an advisor
  will follow up. It never promises an outcome.
- **Patient details are flagged.** If a request names a patient, a date of
  birth or a record number, it is marked so staff handle it with care.
- **Follow-ups are recognised.** When a client replies to an earlier email,
  the record notes that it continues an earlier request, so the advisor
  reads the thread first.

## The outcome

Every request lands in one place, already sorted, with a next step and a
draft reply waiting, and the team only gets pinged for what can't wait.

![A Slack alert in the intake-alerts channel: a Critical regulatory and compliance request from a medical group, with the suggested next step and a link to the record](/case-studies/multichannel-intake/slack-alert.png)

*The alert for a Critical request: a regulator's 14-day deadline about
exposed patient records, with the next step and a link to the record.*

Each channel was tested: a critical web request alerted Slack, a low-urgency
one waited quietly in the table, a staff note from a meeting was logged
under the staff member's name, an email was classified, and a reply to that
email was recognised as a follow-up. Sending the same web request twice was
stopped at the duplicate check.

One known gap, stated plainly: the drafted reply is copied and sent by hand.
An approve-and-send step, like the Slack approval in my
[quote request build](/automation/quote-requests), is the next thing I would
add for a firm running this live.
