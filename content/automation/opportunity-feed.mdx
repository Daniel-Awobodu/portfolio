## The problem

This is an industry build: a complete, working system built to a realistic
brief from a small automation agency.

The team scrolled job boards every day looking for work they could deliver.
Most postings were poor matches, reading them all took hours, and good gigs
still slipped past. They wanted a daily shortlist of the postings worth their
time, each with a one-line reason.

## What I built

A scheduled workflow in n8n that pulls new postings each morning, scores
every one against a short profile of the agency's services, and posts one
digest to Slack.

![The n8n workflow: the daily trigger and settings, the job feed with a health check and a Source Down alert, the duplicate filter, AI scoring, the proof check, saving to Airtable and the Slack digest](/case-studies/opportunity-feed/workflow.png)

*The workflow, from the 10am trigger to the Slack digest. The highlighted
step is the proof check. Click the image to see it full size.*

1. **It runs every morning at 10am.** The agency's profile and scoring rules
   live in one settings step, so changing what counts as a fit takes a
   minute.
2. **It checks the job feed before trusting it.** If the source is down,
   empty or has changed format, the team gets an alert saying today's jobs
   were not checked, instead of a misleading "no jobs today".
3. **It only scores what's new.** Every posting is checked against the
   Airtable log, so nothing is scored or posted twice. Job descriptions are
   cleaned of their web formatting first.
4. **AI scores each posting** against the agency profile: how well the work
   matches the agency's skills, whether a client would hire an agency for it,
   whether the pay works, and any red flags. Before scoring, it must copy
   one passage from the posting, word for word, as proof.
5. **Code checks the proof and decides.** The quote must really appear in
   the posting, and code, not the AI, adds up the final score. A job is a fit
   only with 6 or more out of 10, including at least 3 out of 6 for skills.
6. **One digest goes to Slack**, after everything is saved to Airtable:
   fits, jobs that need a human check, and pitch ideas.

## How it works, explained simply

Think of it as a researcher who reads every job board before you wake up,
and has to show you the exact line that convinced them before recommending
anything.

- **No proof, no post.** If a job scores as a fit but its quote can't be
  found in the posting, it is never posted as a fit. It goes under "Needs a
  human check" instead.
- **Job labels aren't trusted blindly.** Job boards often mislabel roles. If
  the board says full-time but the AI thinks it is project work, the job is
  flagged for a person to check.
- **Red flags cost points.** Pay-to-apply, commission-only pay, talent
  networks posing as clients and spammy posts are labelled and scored down.
- **Hiring posts can become sales leads.** When a company is hiring someone
  to do work by hand that the agency could automate, the digest suggests a
  cold pitch, backed by its own verified quote from the posting.
- **Nothing gets mixed up.** If the number of AI answers doesn't match the
  number of jobs, the run stops instead of guessing which answer belongs to
  which job.
- **Failures are explained in plain words.** A separate workflow posts the
  step that failed and exactly what to do about it.

## The outcome

The agency gets one short message each morning instead of an hour of
scrolling, and every recommendation can be checked against the posting in
seconds.

![The Airtable log: every job with its status, reason, final score and the evidence quote, each marked as verified](/case-studies/opportunity-feed/airtable-log.png)

*Every job in the Airtable log, with its status, reason, score and the
quote behind it, each one verified. Company names are blurred.*

Testing tightened the scoring. On the first run, 15 of 16 real postings were
judged correctly. The miss was a talent marketplace scored as a fit. After
the scoring rules were rewritten, all 16 were judged correctly. A pitch idea
aimed at a company that sells human AI evaluation was stopped by a new rule.
Hand-made test cases proved the safety checks: an invented quote and a
mislabelled full-time job were both flagged, never posted.

One known gap, stated plainly: the free job feed is small, delayed and
mostly full-time roles, so real fits are rare. In the run on 16 real
postings, none were a fit for an agency, and the digest said so. Adding a
freelance marketplace like Upwork as a second source is the next step for an
agency running this live.
