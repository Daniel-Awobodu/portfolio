## The product

Quality-Buy is the online store I run with my business partner. It sells home
goods across Nigeria, and every order is paid on delivery.

Sofa covers are one of its products: stretch covers that make an old sofa look
new and protect a new one. They sell from ₦25,000 for a one-seater to ₦100,000
for a full set.

## The landing page

The ads send people to one page, built for a phone first, because that is
where people see Facebook ads.

What a buyer sees, top to bottom:

- **Pay on delivery and free delivery**, in the very first line, so a buyer
  knows they risk nothing before reading on.
- **A product video and a before and after**, so the change is clear in
  seconds.
- **A price for every size**, with the old price crossed out, and a full set
  as the best value.
- **Numbered designs**, so the order form can ask for one by number.
- **A refund guarantee, then reviews**, some with video.
- **A small commitment fee before dispatch**, explained in plain words, to cut
  down on packages nobody collects.
- **A short order form**: name, address, WhatsApp number, design and size,
  plus a box agreeing to take the confirmation call.

</PhoneScroll>

When a buyer submits the form, a thank-you page loads, and that page tells
Meta an order came in. Those are the orders Ads Manager counts.

## The result

Ads Manager counts **131 orders** from **₦400,275.75** of ad spend on the sofa
cover campaign: **₦3,055.54 per order**. On a product that sells from
₦25,000, that is about an eighth of the cheapest price.

Every order also lands in the store's order database, a form plugin on the
website. The sofa cover form holds 217 entries so far. The same screen shows
the rest of the store: 453 bedcover orders, 227 rug orders and 155 wall art
orders.

![The store's order forms in WordPress, sorted by number of entries. The sofa cover form has 217.](/case-studies/sofa-cover/order-forms.png)

*The store's order forms, most entries first. Each entry is an order form a
buyer filled in.*

Each order then reaches WhatsApp within about 15 minutes, through my
[order alert automation](/automation/order-alerts), so my partner can call the
buyer and confirm while they are still keen. The alerts shown on that page are
sofa cover orders from this form.

## The test that did not work

Not every product sells. In April 2026 we tested a campaign for indoor and
outdoor lights, at ₦8,500 a day. After ₦20,864.18 it had 2 orders, at
₦10,432.09 each: more than three times the cost of a sofa cover order. So we
stopped it, after about two and a half days of budget.

It is the first row in the screenshot at the top of this page. A losing test
stopped early is cheap. The expensive mistake is letting one keep running.

<NextStep>

**Count delivered orders, not just placed ones.** Every order is paid on
delivery, and Meta only sees an order being placed. Marking each order as
delivered or not, and sending that back to Meta, would show the real return on
ad spend, and help Meta find more of the buyers who actually pay.

</NextStep>
